**Document Version:** 1.0.0  
**Target Platform:** Pupil PH Web & Mobile Application (PWA / Responsive Web)  
**Language Specification:** Pure Simple English Only (Relatable Philippine Contexts, Zero Tagalog Translations)  
**Target Audiences:** 
1. Level 1: Pre-School & Kindergarten (Ages 4–6)
2. Level 2: Lower Elementary (Grades 1–3, Ages 6–9)
3. Level 3: Upper Elementary (Grades 4–6, Ages 9–12)

## 1. Executive Overview & Pedagogical Framework

### 1.1 Context and Mission
In many Philippine schools and homes, students interact with digital devices in varied environments:
- School computer laboratories where two or three students often share a single desktop computer.
- Local government-issued student tablets and parent-owned shared smartphones running Android.
- Community computer rental shops and internet cafes.
- Erratic electrical power conditions, where sudden brownouts (power interruptions) can cause unrecoverable loss of unsaved school work.
- Widespread mobile connectivity paired with high exposure to social media, messaging apps, and deceptive SMS/chat scams (such as fake GCash reward links, bogus scholarship raffles, and mobile load lures).

**Pupil PH** delivers foundational digital literacy that empowers every Filipino child and teenager with practical, dignified, and safe computing skills.

### 1.2 Pedagogical Principles
1. **Dignity-First & Age-Appropriate Tone:**
   - Level 1 and Level 2 materials use gentle, warm, playful, and encouraging language.
   - Level 4A (Foundations Bridge) addresses high school students with maturity and respect. It never uses baby talk or childish metaphors, recognizing that an older student learning to use a mouse or keyboard deserves dignity.
2. **Pure Simple English:**
   - Sentences are short, active, and direct (averaging 7 to 14 words per sentence).
   - Technical terms are immediately defined using clear real-world comparisons before abbreviations are introduced.
   - No Tagalog words, expressions, or parallel translations are used, ensuring clean universal comprehension across all regions of the Philippines.
3. **Philippine Everyday Realities:**
   - Brownout survival habits: teaching `Ctrl+S` (Save) as an automatic physical habit.
   - Computer lab hygiene: washing hands after eating snacks (fried snacks, oily food), keeping water tumblers away from power strips and CPU towers, and pushing chairs in.
   - Local scams: identifying fake GCash alerts, free mobile load traps, unsolicited prize notifications, and social media account takeovers.
   - School communication etiquette: addressing teachers with polite greetings, stating grade and section clearly, sending tidy attachments, and avoiding all-caps text.
4. **Multimodal Learning Loop:**
   - Every lesson includes an **On-Screen Card Script**, an **Audio Read-Aloud Voiceover**, a **Visual/Art Description**, an **Actionable Real-Life Tip**, and a **Multiple-Choice Knowledge Check with Instant Corrective Feedback**.

### Module 1.1: Meet the Computer & Tablet

#### Lesson 1.1.1: The Screen (Monitor & Tablet)
- **Target Competency:** Identify the screen on a computer monitor and a tablet; understand that the screen displays pictures, letters, and numbers.
- **Visual & Art Direction:**
  - A friendly cartoon illustration of an open computer lab desk.
  - A cheerful laptop smiling with big kind eyes on its screen.
  - Next to it, a colorful tablet resting gently on a soft desk mat.
  - Glowing soft blue outline highlighting the glass screen area.
- **Step-by-Step Lesson Script:**
  - *Card 1:* "Look at the screen! The screen is like a magic picture window."
  - *Card 2:* "The screen shows us bright pictures, friendly storybooks, and colorful videos."
  - *Card 3:* "We look at the screen with our bright eyes. We keep our face a little step away so our eyes feel comfortable and happy."
- **Audio Read-Aloud Voiceover Script:**
  > "Hello, friend! Welcome to our computer room! Look at the shiny screen right in front of you. It is like a magic picture window. The screen shows you stories, games, and happy colors. Look at the screen with your bright eyes. Keep a little space between your face and the screen so your eyes stay fresh and rested!"
- **Real-Life Philippine Tip:**
  > In your classroom or at home, sit one arm's length away from the screen. If sunlight from the window makes the screen hard to see, ask an adult to close the curtain.
- **Multiple-Choice Quiz:**
  - **Question:** What does the computer screen do?
    - A) It displays pictures, words, and stories for us to see. *(Correct)*
    - B) It washes our hands with water.
    - C) It cooks warm rice for lunch.
    - D) It plays under the rain.
  - **Correct Answer:** A
  - **Feedback (Correct):** "Wonderful job! Yes, the screen shows us pictures, words, and fun lessons to see!"
  - **Feedback (Incorrect):** "Let us try again! The screen is the glass window that shows us pictures and words. Choose the answer that says it displays pictures and words!"

#### Lesson 1.1.2: The Keyboard & Mouse
- **Target Competency:** Recognize the keyboard as the tool for letters and numbers, and the mouse as the tool that points and clicks.
- **Visual & Art Direction:**
  - Split visual: on the left, a colorful keyboard with friendly smiling letter buttons.
  - On the right, a rounded computer mouse shaped like a friendly little creature with two distinct ears (left button and right button) and a glowing tail (cord).
  - An animated arrow (pointer) hopping joyfully across the screen.
- **Step-by-Step Lesson Script:**
  - *Card 1:* "This is the keyboard! It has many small buttons called keys. The keys have letters and numbers on them."
  - *Card 2:* "When you press a key gently, that letter appears on your screen!"
  - *Card 3:* "This is the mouse! It rests flat on the table. When you slide the mouse, a little arrow moves on the screen."
  - *Card 4:* "The mouse has buttons. When you press the left button gently, it makes a quiet *click* sound!"
- **Audio Read-Aloud Voiceover Script:**
  > "Meet your two new helpers: the keyboard and the mouse! The keyboard is full of little buttons called keys. Each key has a letter or a number. When you press a key gently with your finger, that letter pops up on the screen! Now look at the mouse sitting on the table. When your hand moves the mouse, a little arrow glides across the screen. Press the button gently: click! You did it!"
- **Real-Life Philippine Tip:**
  > When holding the computer mouse in the school lab, rest your whole hand flat and relaxed. Do not squeeze the mouse tightly like a toy car. Keep it calm and flat on the mousepad.
- **Multiple-Choice Quiz:**
  - **Question:** Which tool has letter buttons that let you type your name?
    - A) The computer mouse
    - B) The keyboard *(Correct)*
    - C) The power plug
    - D) The headphone cable
  - **Correct Answer:** B
  - **Feedback (Correct):** "Spot on! The keyboard has all the letter and number keys so you can type your name!"
  - **Feedback (Incorrect):** "Not quite! Remember, the keyboard is the flat board filled with letters and numbers. Give it another try!"

#### Lesson 1.1.3: Touching Gently with Clean Hands
- **Target Competency:** Demonstrate clean hand hygiene and gentle touching on touchscreen tablets and computer accessories.
- **Visual & Art Direction:**
  - Left panel: A happy child washing hands with soap and water, then drying them with a clean towel before touching a tablet.
  - Right panel: A tablet screen showing gentle fingertip taps with star sparkles; an illustration showing a red "No" symbol over sticky snack crumbs or drinks near the tablet.
- **Step-by-Step Lesson Script:**
  - *Card 1:* "Computers and tablets love clean, dry hands!"
  - *Card 2:* "Before using the computer or tablet, always wash your hands with soap and dry them well."
  - *Card 3:* "Never eat snacks or drink juice while touching the computer. Sticky fingers make the keys stick and make the screen dirty."
  - *Card 4:* "Touch the screen softly like a butterfly landing on a flower. Never hit, tap hard, or poke the glass."
- **Audio Read-Aloud Voiceover Script:**
  > "Our tablets and computers are wonderful tools, and we take good care of them! Always wash your hands before you play or learn. If your fingers are sticky from afternoon snacks, the tablet screen gets messy and hard to read. When you tap the screen, be very gentle! Tap softly like a butterfly touching a leaf. Never bang on the screen or slam the keys."
- **Real-Life Philippine Tip:**
  > If you just finished eating fried snacks like banana cue or chips during recess, wash your hands with soap before touching the school tablet. Oil and sugar make the tablet screen blurry and slippery!
- **Multiple-Choice Quiz:**
  - **Question:** What should you do before touching the school tablet or computer?
    - A) Wipe your hands on your clothes after eating sticky snacks.
    - B) Wash your hands with soap and dry them completely. *(Correct)*
    - C) Hold a cup of cold juice in your other hand.
    - D) Press the screen as hard as you can with a pencil.
  - **Correct Answer:** B
  - **Feedback (Correct):** "Great choice! Clean and dry hands keep our tablets and keyboards working smoothly for everyone!"
  - **Feedback (Incorrect):** "Oops! Sticky fingers can hurt the computer. Always wash your hands with soap and dry them first. Try again!"

### Module 1.2: Important Buttons & Healthy Habits

#### Lesson 1.2.1: The Power Symbol
- **Target Competency:** Recognize the international Power symbol (a circle broken by a vertical line at the top) and know to ask an adult before pressing it.
- **Visual & Art Direction:**
  - A large, bright green glowing circle with a vertical line through the top: the standard Power symbol `⏻`.
  - Next to it, an attentive cartoon puppy looking at the button with a friendly thought bubble showing a smiling teacher smiling and nodding.
- **Step-by-Step Lesson Script:**
  - *Card 1:* "Look closely at this special shape. It is a round circle with a straight line standing up at the top."
  - *Card 2:* "This special shape is called the **Power Symbol**."
  - *Card 3:* "The power symbol wakes up the computer when it is sleeping, and puts it to rest when school is over."
  - *Card 4:* "Always ask your teacher or parent before pressing the power button. We do not press it over and over like a game."
- **Audio Read-Aloud Voiceover Script:**
  > "Do you see this neat little symbol? It looks like a circle with a small line at the top. That is the Power Symbol! It wakes the computer up so you can learn, and it helps the computer go to sleep safely when you are done. Because the power button is so important, always ask your teacher or a parent before you press it. Good job looking!"
- **Real-Life Philippine Tip:**
  > In your school computer lab, never press the power button on your seatmate's computer. Only touch your own computer when your teacher gives the signal to start.
- **Multiple-Choice Quiz:**
  - **Question:** What does the circle with a straight line at the top mean?
    - A) It is the Power button. *(Correct)*
    - B) It means you can eat a snack now.
    - C) It is the volume button to sing loud.
    - D) It means the computer is broken forever.
  - **Correct Answer:** A
  - **Feedback (Correct):** "Super! That circle with a line at the top is the universal Power symbol!"
  - **Feedback (Incorrect):** "Take another look! That circle with the line at the top wakes up the computer. It is the Power button. Select the Power button!"

#### Lesson 1.2.2: Volume & The Back Button
- **Target Competency:** Locate and understand the purpose of the Volume buttons (speaker icons) and the Back button (left arrow).
- **Visual & Art Direction:**
  - Two distinct cards:
    - Card A: A speaker cone with sound waves (loud / soft volume icons `🔊`, `🔉`, `🔈`).
    - Card B: A bold curved arrow pointing to the left `⬅️` inside a friendly circular bubble labeled "Back".
- **Step-by-Step Lesson Script:**
  - *Card 1:* "Computers can talk, play songs, and tell stories through their speakers."
  - *Card 2:* "The button with sound waves is the **Volume** button. It makes the sound softer or louder."
  - *Card 3:* "In a room with other students, we keep the volume soft so we do not disturb our friends."
  - *Card 4:* "The arrow pointing to the left `⬅️` is the **Back Button**. If you open the wrong page, tap the back arrow to return to your previous page!"
- **Audio Read-Aloud Voiceover Script:**
  > "Listen closely! The computer has a voice. When you want the voice to whisper or speak clearly, look for the little speaker icon. That is the Volume button! Keep it at a nice, calm sound level so your classmates can also hear their own lessons. And if you ever get lost or tap the wrong picture, do not worry! Just look for the arrow pointing back to the left. Tap it once, and you are back where you started!"
- **Real-Life Philippine Tip:**
  > When using a tablet in a quiet classroom or at home while someone is resting, check the volume before pressing play. Turn it down low or plug in child-friendly earphones.
- **Multiple-Choice Quiz:**
  - **Question:** What should you tap if you open the wrong game or page and want to go back?
    - A) The arrow pointing to the left (Back Button) *(Correct)*
    - B) The power plug under the table
    - C) The screen brightness slider
    - D) The table surface
  - **Correct Answer:** A
  - **Feedback (Correct):** "Excellent! The left arrow is the Back button. It takes you right back to where you were!"
  - **Feedback (Incorrect):** "Remember, the arrow pointing to the left brings you back to your previous page. Pick the left arrow!"

#### Lesson 1.2.3: Sitting Properly & Asking an Adult
- **Target Competency:** Practice healthy sitting posture (back straight, feet flat) and remember the golden rule: ask an adult if anything strange or scary appears.
- **Visual & Art Direction:**
  - An illustration comparing two postures:
    - A child slouching with neck bent close to a screen (marked with a gentle dotted red guideline showing neck strain).
    - A child sitting tall with shoulders relaxed, back straight against the chair, feet resting on the floor or footstool, smiling happily at a desk.
  - A friendly teacher wearing an ID lanyard bending down to help a student who raised their hand.
- **Step-by-Step Lesson Script:**
  - *Card 1:* "Our bodies like good habits! Sit up straight like a happy sunflower reaching for the sun."
  - *Card 2:* "Keep both feet resting flat on the floor or footstool. Keep your shoulders relaxed."
  - *Card 3:* "Give your eyes a break! Look away from the screen every few minutes and look out the window at green trees."
  - *Card 4:* "The Golden Rule: If a strange picture, scary sound, or confusing message pops up on your screen, do not tap it. Raise your hand and tell your teacher or an adult right away!"
- **Audio Read-Aloud Voiceover Script:**
  > "Let us check your posture! Sit tall and comfortable. Keep your back straight, your shoulders relaxed, and your feet flat on the floor. Remember to blink your eyes and look across the room at the trees outside to keep your eyes happy. And here is our most important rule: if you ever see something strange, confusing, or scary on your screen, do not touch it! Raise your hand and ask your teacher or an adult. They are always happy to help you!"
- **Real-Life Philippine Tip:**
  > In school computer labs, chairs can sometimes spin. Keep your chair still and facing forward so you do not bump into your seatmate's desk or kick computer cables underneath.
- **Multiple-Choice Quiz:**
  - **Question:** What should you do if an unexpected, strange pop-up message appears on your screen?
    - A) Tap random buttons quickly.
    - B) Raise your hand and ask your teacher or an adult for help. *(Correct)*
    - C) Hide under the desk.
    - D) Turn off the classroom lights.
  - **Correct Answer:** B
  - **Feedback (Correct):** "Spot on! Raising your hand and asking an adult is always the safest and smartest choice!"
  - **Feedback (Incorrect):** "Do not tap strange things! Always ask your teacher or parent to check it for you. Choose the option to raise your hand and ask an adult!"

### Module 2.1: Using the Mouse, Touchpad & Cursor

#### Lesson 2.1.1: Moving the Pointer & Single-Click
- **Target Competency:** Guide the mouse cursor across the screen smoothly and execute a single left-click to select items and activate buttons.
- **Visual & Art Direction:**
  - A detailed top-down view of a two-button mouse with a scroll wheel.
  - The left button is highlighted in bright sunny yellow, labeled "Index Finger (Left Click)".
  - On the computer screen, the white arrow pointer hovers directly over a blue button labeled "Start Story". A soft ring animation pulses outward when clicked.
- **Step-by-Step Lesson Script:**
  - *Card 1:* "When you slide your mouse on the mousepad, the white arrow on the screen moves in the exact same direction. That arrow is your **Pointer** (or **Cursor**)."
  - *Card 2:* "Your index finger rests naturally on the **Left Mouse Button**. This is the main button you will use most of the time."
  - *Card 3:* "To **Click**, press the left button down gently once, then release it immediately: *Click!*"
  - *Card 4:* "Moving your pointer on top of an object is called **Hovering**. Clicking it tells the computer: 'I choose this one!'"
- **Audio Read-Aloud Voiceover Script:**
  > "Welcome to cursor training! Place your hand comfortably over your mouse. As you slide your mouse to the left, your pointer slides to the left. Move it up, and your pointer moves up! Look at your index finger. It rests right on the left mouse button. When you want to choose an option or press a button on screen, move your pointer over the button, press down gently, and let go right away. That quick *click* tells the computer what you want to do!"
- **Real-Life Philippine Tip:**
  > If your mouse pointer stops moving or jumps around, check the bottom of the mouse. Sometimes dust from the school desk collects near the red sensor light. Ask your teacher for a clean cloth to wipe the desk.
- **Multiple-Choice Quiz:**
  - **Question:** Which finger normally rests on the primary left mouse button used for clicking buttons?
    - A) Your thumb
    - B) Your index finger (pointer finger) *(Correct)*
    - C) Your pinky finger
    - D) Your elbow
  - **Correct Answer:** B
  - **Feedback (Correct):** "Exactly right! Your index finger rests comfortably on the left button for easy clicking!"
  - **Feedback (Incorrect):** "Check your hand position! Your index finger (the finger you point with) rests on the left button. Let us select that answer!"

#### Lesson 2.1.2: Double-Click & Drag-and-Drop
- **Target Competency:** Perform a rapid double-click to open folders and applications; execute click-hold-drag-and-release to move screen objects.
- **Visual & Art Direction:**
  - Two animated diagrams:
    - Diagram 1: Two fast musical notes `♪ ♪` over the left mouse button showing the rhythm: *click-click!*
    - Diagram 2: The mouse cursor clicking on a yellow folder icon, holding it down while moving across the screen, and releasing it inside a blue box labeled "Art Folder".
- **Step-by-Step Lesson Script:**
  - *Card 1:* "A **Double-Click** means pressing the left mouse button twice very quickly: *Click-click!*"
  - *Card 2:* "Double-clicking is like knocking twice on a door: *Knock-knock!* It opens folders, games, and school programs on your desktop."
  - *Card 3:* "To **Drag and Drop**: Move your pointer over an item. Press and hold the left mouse button down."
  - *Card 4:* "While holding the button down, slide the mouse to move the item across the screen. When the item reaches its new spot, lift your finger up to **Drop** it!"
- **Audio Read-Aloud Voiceover Script:**
  > "Now let us learn two powerful mouse skills! First is the double-click. Press your left button two times fast: click-click! Think of it like knocking twice on your classroom door. This is how you open folders and programs. Second is drag-and-drop. Point to an item, hold the left button down tight, and glide your mouse across the table. Watch the item move with you! When it is in the right place, let go of the button. You just dropped it safely into place!"
- **Real-Life Philippine Tip:**
  > If your double-click is too slow, the computer might think you made two separate single clicks. Keep your hand steady and tap twice briskly without lifting your hand off the mouse.
- **Multiple-Choice Quiz:**
  - **Question:** How do you drop an object after dragging it across the screen?

    - C) Press the spacebar five times.
    - D) Unplug the keyboard.
  - **Correct Answer:** A
  - **Feedback (Correct):** "Spot on! When you lift your finger off the mouse button, the item lands right where you placed it!"
  - **Feedback (Incorrect):** "To drop an object, simply release the mouse button you were holding. Try again!"

  - *Card 5:* "On a laptop touchpad, place two fingers together gently and slide them up or down to scroll smoothly!"

Roll the wheel toward yourself to slide down the page and see what happens next in the story. Roll it forward to climb back to the top. If you are using a laptop, place two fingers on the touchpad and slide them together. It works like magic!"
- **Real-Life Philippine Tip:**
  > When reading digital textbooks for Science or English on a school tablet, you can also swipe your index finger up and down smoothly on the glass to scroll.

  - **Correct Answer:** A
  - **Feedback (Correct):** "Bravo! The scroll wheel lets you roll up and down long stories easily!"

#### 4.1.1 UI Layout & Visual Assets
- **Canvas View:** 16:9 full-screen responsive SVG/Canvas interactive field.

#### 4.1.2 Interaction Mechanics & State Machine
- **State 1: `INTRO_PROMPT`**
  - Audio plays: *"Let us practice gentle tapping! Look for the yellow butterfly and tap it softly with one finger."*

- **State 2: `WAITING_FOR_TAP`**
  - Single tap event listener active (`touchstart` / `pointerdown`).
  - Target radius: Generous 64px circular touch hitbox around sprite center (optimized for small children's fingers).
- **State 3: `SUCCESS_TRIGGER`**
  - Upon valid single tap: Target pauses, does a happy little 360-degree twirl, emits 6 colorful star particles, and plays a sweet xylophone chime.
  - Audio prompt: *"Gentle touch! Wonderful job!"*
  - Star counter increments (`+1 Star`).
- **State 4: `RAPID_SPAM_DETECTED` (Edge Case Handler)**
  - If 5+ rapid uncoordinated touch events fire in < 1000ms:
  - Sprites pause movement; friendly animated character appears with soft audio: *"Take a breath, friend! Just one gentle tap with one finger."*
  - Background glows with calming lavender light for 2 seconds before resuming.

#### 4.4.1 UI Layout & Visual Wireframe
- **Interactive Document Canvas:** A responsive word-processor-like interface with an active text buffer, cursor caret, and selection highlights.
- **On-Screen Keystroke Visualizer:** Displays real-time mechanical keycaps at the bottom of the screen that light up when the physical keys are pressed (`Ctrl`, `Shift`, `Alt`, letter keys).

#### 4.4.2 Interaction Mechanics & State Machine
- **Keystroke Interceptor:** JavaScript `keydown` listener capturing `e.ctrlKey`, `e.metaKey`, `e.key`, and preventing default browser actions for captured combinations (such as preventing the browser from attempting to save the actual webpage when the student presses `Ctrl+S` inside the simulator).
- **Interactive Sandbox Scenarios:**
  - *Scenario A (Undo Save):* A digital character accidentally deletes three paragraphs of history research. The student must press `Ctrl+Z` within 5 seconds to undo the deletion and save the day.
  - *Scenario B (Find & Replace):* A 10-page text contains the misspelled word "Philipines". Student must press `Ctrl+F`, type "Philipines", and find all 6 occurrences to fix them.
  - *Scenario C (Clipboard Relay):* Cut (`Ctrl+X`) three misplaced bibliography entries from the top of the article and paste (`Ctrl+V`) them into the bibliography section at the bottom.

#### 4.5.2 Realistic Philippine Scenarios Database
1. **Case 1 (GCash OTP Scam):**
   - *Message:* "Hi Ma'am/Sir, this is GCash Support. An unauthorized login was detected in Davao. Please reply with the 6-digit OTP sent to your inbox to cancel the transfer."
   - *Verdict:* **SCAM**.
   - *Explanation:* Legitimate support staff will NEVER ask you to text or read your OTP.
2. **Case 2 (Official School Portal Notification):**
   - *Message:* "From: DEPED-QC. Good day. First Quarter Periodical Examinations will begin on October 28, 2026. Review materials are available at your school portal: https://qc.deped.gov.ph."
   - *Verdict:* **SAFE**.
   - *Explanation:* Comes from an official broadcast channel, uses a clean `.gov.ph` domain, and does not ask for passwords, OTPs, or money.
3. **Case 3 (Free Mobile Load Raffle):**
   - *Message:* "Congratulations SIM# 0918***! You have won 1,000 PHP free Smart/Globe load in our mid-year consumer raffle. Claim now at http://bit.ly/claim-free-load-ph2026."
   - *Verdict:* **SCAM**.
   - *Explanation:* Unsolicited raffle win for a contest never entered; uses a shortened URL to disguise a phishing page.
4. **Case 4 (Classroom Teacher Submission Reminder):**
   - *Message:* "Email from: `santos.m@manilacityschool.edu.ph`. Subject: Math Project Submission Deadline. Good day class, please submit your Math Project PDF through our Google Classroom stream by Friday 5:00 PM."
   - *Verdict:* **SAFE**.
   - *Explanation:* Verified institutional `.edu.ph` email, clear context, no threats, and standard academic instruction.
5. **Case 5 (Fake Social Media Account Security Alert):**
   - *Message:* "Facebook Security Team: Your profile has been reported for copyright violation. Login within 24 hours at http://facebook-verify-profile.online/auth or your account will be permanently deleted."
   - *Verdict:* **SCAM**.
   - *Explanation:* Phishing domain `.online` attempting to harvest social media login credentials through fear.

### 5.2 Analytics & Learning Telemetry Events
The frontend application will dispatch standard telemetry events to track mastery and identify areas where students need further practice:
